\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The effective gravitational kernel of the previous version (v4) describes the ratio of observed to baryonic acceleration in bound systems with $\Keff = 1+\lambda e^{-x}/(1-e^{-x})^{n}$, $x=g_{\rm bar}/\gdag(z)$, $\gdag=cH(z)/2\pi$ and $n(\xi)=\tfrac12+A\xi$. Its radial extension, an exponent modulation $w^{-\alpha(\xi)}$ with two extra parameters calibrated on a subset of the same data, was left as a semi-predictive result. Here the radial dependence is replaced by a scale shift $s=[\Delta(r)/\Delta_{500}]^{\,p\,\Theta(\xi)}$, $\Delta/\Delta_{500}=\bar\rho(<r)/500\rho_c$, with a single radial amplitude $p$ and a logistic switch $\Theta$ in the compactness $\xi$ (threshold $\xi_c$, width $m$) that turns the term on in groups and clusters and off in galaxies. On the 39 X-COP radial points of v4 the rms falls from 0.037 to 0.021 dex at $p=0.5$ (0.016 at the fitted $p=0.45$); the cluster-bootstrap 95\% interval of the paired difference is 0.007--0.024 dex, and the comparison is in-sample. With $p=0.5$ frozen, other samples were predicted without refitting. In ten Vikhlinin et al.\ (2006) clusters the ratio $\fbar(r_{2500})/\fbar(r_{500})$ is predicted with $\ln({\rm obs/pred})=-0.10\pm0.06$ (v4 law $-0.61\pm0.21$; no radial term $-1.29\pm0.07$), and in nine X-COP clusters $\fbar(r_{200})/\fbar(r_{500})$ with $+0.03\pm0.04$ (v4 law $+0.02\pm0.06$; none $+0.87\pm0.06$). The best-fit $p$ nevertheless differs between samples (0.45--0.60) by more than the statistical errors, so $p=0.52\pm0.07$ carries a systematic uncertainty of order 20\% in the predicted baryon fraction; a hydrostatic bias of 0--30\% moves $p$ by less than 0.1. The galaxy--group switch is loosely constrained: widths $m\ge3$ and thresholds $0.09\lesssim\xi_c\lesssim0.16$ fit comparably, and a smooth power law $p(\xi)$ without threshold is disfavoured by $\Delta(-2\ln L)\simeq50$. The evolution $\gdag\propto H(z)$ is preferred by measured gas fractions (a frozen $\gdag$ is rejected by $\Delta\chi^2=57$ and 48), whereas median KMOS$^{\rm 3D}$ galaxies at $z\simeq0.9$--2.3 favour a weaker evolution, a difference within the $\sim10\%$ systematics. We document unresolved tensions: the baryon fraction at $r_{2500}$ rises with mass more steeply than observed, the absolute normalisation differs by $\sim20\%$ between the Chandra and XMM samples, and the high-redshift galaxy trend. Applied to linear cosmological perturbations the kernel boosts gravity by factors $10^2$--$10^3$; a density-based damper restores $\Lambda$CDM growth only by postulating the plateau $K_c=\Omega_m/\Omega_b$. The program is an explicitly phenomenological map of bound systems, comparable to $\Lambda$CDM in clusters, parsimonious but poorer in fit in galaxies, and silent on the CMB.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The kernel proposed in the first paper of this program (v4, hereafter Paper~I) maps the baryonic acceleration $g_{\rm bar}$ of a bound system to its observed acceleration, $g_{\rm obs}=\Keff\,g_{\rm bar}$, using a transition scale $\gdag(z)=cH(z)/2\pi$ and an exponent $n$ that depends on the equilibrium geometry through the compactness $\xi=g_{500}/\gdag(z)$. Paper~I tested it on SPARC disk galaxies, 23 groups, 13 X-COP clusters and 23 CLASH clusters and listed its own limitations. Four of them are the subject of this work.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0.1em]
\item \emph{The radial law was semi-predictive.} The factor $w^{-\alpha(\xi)}$, with $\alpha(\xi)=0.355(1-e^{-\xi/0.052})$, used two parameters derived earlier from a subset of eight X-COP clusters, and the 39-point test of Paper~I partially overlaps that calibration. Paper~I lists validation on resolved profiles, with a falsification criterion of 0.10 dex, as an open item.
\item \emph{The evidence for $\gdag\propto H(z)$ rested on CLASH gas fractions estimated from a scaling relation.} Paper~I itself discusses whether part of the residual correlation is algebraic.
\item \emph{Groups were 23 and no variable switched the radial term between galaxies and clusters.}
\item \emph{No test at high redshift and no treatment of cosmological scales} were given; the extension was declared outside the scope.
\end{enumerate}

We (i) replace the radial law by a scale shift with a single amplitude and compare it with the v4 law on the same data and, with the amplitude frozen, on other samples (Sect.~\ref{sec:radial}); (ii) test $\gdag(z)$ with measured gas fractions and with $z\simeq1$--2 galaxies (Sect.~\ref{sec:gz}); (iii) introduce a switch in $\xi$ connecting galaxies to groups and clusters, test whether it must be a sharp step, and derive its redshift dependence (Sect.~\ref{sec:switch}); (iv) compare with $\Lambda$CDM (Sect.~\ref{sec:lcdm}); (v) document the tensions (Sect.~\ref{sec:tensions}); and (vi) examine what happens when the kernel is applied to cosmological perturbations and how a density-based damper behaves (Sect.~\ref{sec:cosmo}). Section~\ref{sec:disc} states what the program does and does not claim, and lists falsifiable tests.

\section{The kernel and its radial extension}
\label{sec:model}
\subsection{Definition}
For a bound, spherically or axially symmetric system in equilibrium,
\begin{equation}
\Keff = 1+\frac{\lambda\,e^{-x/s}}{\left(1-e^{-x/s}\right)^{n}},\qquad x=\frac{g_{\rm bar}}{\gdag(z)},\qquad \gdag(z)=\frac{cH(z)}{2\pi},
\label{eq:K}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda=0.999$, $H_0=70\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$, $\Omega_m=0.3$, and the exponent law of Paper~I,
\begin{equation}
n(\xi)=\tfrac12+A\,\xi,\qquad A=0.4068,\qquad \xi=\frac{g_{500}}{\gdag(z)}=\frac{2\pi\,G M_{500}}{c\,H(z)\,R_{500}^2}.
\label{eq:n}
\end{equation}
For $s=1$ and $\xi\to0$ this is the disk-galaxy kernel ($n=1/2$) with the baryonic Tully--Fisher relation $v_f^4=\lambda^2\gdag G M_b$ as a structural consequence. The compactness $\xi$ scales as $(MH)^{1/3}$.

\subsection{Radial term}
Paper~I modulated the exponent, $n\to n(\xi)\,w^{-\alpha(\xi)}$ with $w=r/R_{500}$. Here the exponent is kept at its $w=1$ value and the radial dependence enters through the scale $s$,
\begin{equation}
s=\left[\frac{\Delta(r)}{\Delta_{500}}\right]^{p\,\Theta(\xi)},\qquad \frac{\Delta(r)}{\Delta_{500}}=\frac{\bar\rho(<r)}{500\,\rho_c(z)}=\frac{M(<r)}{M_{500}}\left(\frac{R_{500}}{r}\right)^{3}.
\label{eq:s}
\end{equation}
By definition $\Delta/\Delta_{500}=1$ at $r_{500}$, $5$ at $r_{2500}$ and $0.4$ at $r_{200}$, so $p$ is the only radial amplitude and the $w=1$ calibration of Paper~I is untouched. $\Theta(\xi)=[1+(\xi_c/\xi)^m]^{-1}$ is a logistic switch in $\ln\xi$. Sect.~\ref{sec:switch} shows that the data constrain neither its sharpness nor its location tightly ($m\ge3$, $0.09\lesssim\xi_c\lesssim0.16$); the fiducial values are $m=4$, $\xi_c=0.11$. For clusters ($\xi\ge0.34$) $\Theta\ge0.98$ for every acceptable choice, so the cluster results below do not depend on it; systems with $\xi\ll\xi_c$ have $s=1$.

\subsection{Self-consistent baryon fraction}
In a system with total enclosed mass $M(<r)$ and baryonic fraction $f=M_{\rm bar}/M$, $g_{\rm obs}=GM/r^2$ and $x=f\,g_{\rm obs}/\gdag$; the identity $K=g_{\rm obs}/g_{\rm bar}=1/f$ gives the equation $f\,K(f\,g_{\rm obs}/\gdag)=1$, solved for $f$. The prediction is a baryon fraction, which is compared with the observed gas plus stellar fraction. Because the observable depends on the measured total mass, hydrostatic masses are interpreted as the effective dynamical mass $g_{\rm obs}r^2/G$; they are unbiased with respect to the gravity law if the gas is in equilibrium in the potential of Eq.~(\ref{eq:K}) (Sect.~\ref{sec:tensions}).

\subsection{Independent data}
\paragraph{Vikhlinin et al.\ (2006), $r_{2500}$.} For the ten clusters with cumulative $\fgas$ at both radii ($\xi=0.23$--0.59, $M_{500}=7.4\times10^{13}$--$1.1\times10^{15}\,\Msun$, $w=r_{2500}/r_{500}=0.39$--0.48) the control at $r_{500}$, where the radial term plays no role, gives obs/pred $=0.95$ (rms 0.13 in $\ln$). At $r_{2500}$ without a radial term obs/pred is 0.26 (rms$_{\ln}$ 1.35, predicted $\fbar(r_{2500})/\fbar(r_{500})=3.37$); with the v4 law 0.45 (rms$_{\ln}$ 0.90, ratio 1.95, and one of the ten clusters has no solution because $n>1$ inside $w\simeq0.45$); with the scale shift 0.84 (rms$_{\ln}$ 0.23, ratio 1.00). The observed ratio is 0.90. The v4 law, which scales the exponent as $w^{-\alpha}$, drives $n$ beyond 1 at $w<1$ for the highest $\xi$, where the kernel no longer has a finite baryon fraction; the scale shift cannot do this because it multiplies $x$ rather than the exponent. At the criterion proposed in Paper~I (0.10 dex), the v4 law with its published $\alpha(\xi)$ fails on this sample (we did not re-optimise $\alpha$).

\paragraph{X-COP out to $r_{200}$.} Ettori et al.\ (2019, Table 2) give $\fgas$ and $M_{\rm HSE}$ at $r_{500}$ and $r_{200}$ for 12 clusters. After adding stars (Kravtsov scaling, $\varphi=1$) the ratio $\fbar(r_{200})/\fbar(r_{500})$ is observed to be 1.03 (median); the scale shift with $p=0.5$ predicts 0.98 (all 12) or 0.97 (the nine clusters left after excluding the mergers A2319, A3266 and ZW1215), against 0.97 for the v4 law (rms of $\ln$[obs/pred ratio] 0.157 against 0.120 for the new term) and 0.42 without a radial term. The mass trend of this ratio is $-0.12$ per dex observed and $-0.11$ predicted. The mass-dependent exponent $p(\xi)\propto\xi^{0.37}$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:tensions}) predicts $+0.11$ and is disfavoured. At $r_{500}$ itself, the X-COP sample has obs/pred $=1.17$ (rms 0.19 in $\ln$) while the Vikhlinin sample gives 0.95 (Sect.~\ref{sec:tensions}).

\paragraph{Gain of the radial term.} Without the radial term the kernel overestimates the baryon fraction at $r_{2500}$ by a factor 3--4 (obs/pred 0.27, rms$_{\ln}$ 1.27 over 12 clusters) and underestimates it at $r_{200}$ by a factor 2.4 (ratio 0.42 against 1.03). With $p=0.5$ the rms falls to 0.225 at $r_{2500}$ and 0.12 at $r_{200}$. In galaxies $\xi<\xi_c$, so $s=1$ and the term is inactive: the gain belongs to groups and clusters. The test shows that the term is necessary and that $[\Delta/\Delta_{500}]^p$ describes it with one parameter; it does not show that this is the only possible form, and $p\simeq0.5$ was calibrated on samples that partially overlap (A1795 and A2029 are in both Vikhlinin and X-COP; see Sect.~\ref{sec:frozen} for the held-out comparison).

\paragraph{Zero-point-free ratio test.} Dividing out the control at $r_{500}$ of each sample (where $p$ plays no role: $-0.05\pm0.04$ for Vikhlinin and $+0.16\pm0.02$ for Ettori) gives the mean of $\ln$ of the ratio between the observed and the predicted $\fbar(r)/\fbar(r_{500})$. At $r_{2500}$ in ten Vikhlinin clusters: $-1.29\pm0.07$ (no radial term), $-0.61\pm0.21$ (v4), $-0.24\pm0.06$ ($p=0.45$), $-0.10\pm0.06$ ($p=0.5$). At $r_{200}$ in nine X-COP clusters: $+0.87\pm0.06$, $+0.02\pm0.06$, $+0.10\pm0.04$, $+0.03\pm0.04$ (errors are standard errors over clusters). The new term is clearly better than v4 inside $r_{500}$ (Vikhlinin, groups, Allen, where v4 loses solutions or diverges) and equivalent to it between $r_{500}$ and $r_{200}$, where v4 is calibrated.

\paragraph{Dispersion of $p$.} The best-fit amplitudes range from 0.45 to 0.60 and their bootstrap intervals do not overlap (X-COP 0.40--0.50 against Ettori 0.60--0.65), so the scatter is systematic, not statistical: the five values have mean 0.52 and standard deviation 0.07. Near $r_{2500}$ and $r_{200}$ a change of 0.05 in $p$ shifts the predicted $\fbar$ by about 0.13 in $\ln$ (Vikhlinin: $-0.28\to-0.15$ from $p=0.45$ to 0.50), so the amplitude is known to $\sim\pm20\%$ in the predicted baryon fraction. It is not a constant of nature at the level of the current data; sources are the hydrostatic bias (Sect.~\ref{sec:tensions}), the instrument zero points, and the unmeasured stellar mass.

\paragraph{What is, and is not, held out.} $p$ was fixed on X-COP before the other samples were evaluated, but the tests are not blind: variants of the model (a mass-dependent $p(\xi)$, Sect.~\ref{sec:tensions}) were evaluated on the same data, the Kravtsov stellar treatment of the groups was adopted after the 9\% case was inspected, and the samples overlap. A blind test requires data not used here. The frozen prescription (parameters and code) is archived with this paper (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}) so that it can be applied to new samples before they are examined.

\section{Evolution of $\gdag$}
\label{sec:gz}
We parametrise $\gdag(z)=\gdag(0)\,E(z)^{\alpha}$, with $\alpha=1$ the prediction $\gdag=cH/2\pi$ and $\alpha=0$ a frozen scale. In clusters $\alpha$ enters both $x$ and $\xi$, so a change of $\alpha$ moves the exponent as well as the transition.

\section{Connecting galaxies to clusters: the $\xi$ switch}
\label{sec:switch}
The radial term cannot be applied to galaxies: in SPARC an exploratory $s=(\Delta/\Delta_{\rm out})^p$, with $\Delta$ taken from the rotation curve itself, degrades the rms from 0.148 to 0.158, 0.235 and 0.468 dex for $p=0.25$, 0.5 and 1, and the global fit favours $p\simeq0.09$. A switch based on the local density does not separate the regimes, because inner cluster radii reach galactic densities; the discriminator must be a property of the system. The compactness $\xi$ is such a property: SPARC haloes have $\xi\simeq0.004$--0.094 (under two halo mass assumptions, A: $M_{500}=M_b/0.10$; B: a stellar-to-halo relation, so $\xi$ for galaxies is imputed, not measured), the 43 groups $\xi=0.136$--0.29, X-COP $0.34$--0.54 and the Allen/Mantz clusters $0.40$--0.85. No object lies in $0.094<\xi<0.136$. A saturating switch such as $1-e^{-\xi/0.052}$, the form inherited from $\alpha(\xi)$ in Paper~I, turns the term on partially at galactic $\xi$ and was found incompatible with the SPARC residuals.

\paragraph{Must the switch be a sharp step?} We fitted $\Theta=[1+(\xi_c/\xi)^m]^{-1}$ with free $(\xi_c,m,p)$ jointly to the X-COP points, the SPARC galaxies and the 43 groups (Kravtsov stars, $\varphi=0.7$), weighting the three samples by their number of independent units (13 clusters, 141 galaxies, 43 groups; weighting by points would let the 2803 SPARC points dominate the likelihood). A smooth power law $p(\xi)=p_0(\xi/0.4)^q$ without threshold, with as many parameters as a step, is disfavoured by $\Delta(-2\ln L)=42$--48 with respect to the step and 46--53 with respect to $m=4$: the galaxies need $p\simeq0$ and the clusters 0.45, and the groups lie between, which a power law cannot reproduce without degrading the clusters (rms 0.024--0.026 against 0.016). \paragraph{Dependence on the stellar mass of groups.} The group amplitude is set by the unmeasured stellar mass inside $r_{2500}$. With Kravtsov stars ($\varphi=0.7$) the groups give $p=0.45$ [0.39, 0.50], consistent with the clusters; with a stellar mass of 9\% of the gas (first draft) they give $p=0.80$ [0.76, 0.85] and the joint fit then degrades the clusters from 0.016 to 0.10 dex. The consistency of $p$ across regimes therefore holds for the Kravtsov relation and fails for the lower stellar masses, and groups cannot yet arbitrate.

\section{Tensions and tests that did not close}
\label{sec:tensions}
\paragraph{(1) Baryon fraction against mass at $r_{2500}$.} The kernel predicts $\fbar(r_{2500})$ rising with $M_{2500}$ by $+0.35$ per dex, against a measured mass slope of the observed fraction of $-0.04\pm0.09$ (Allen) and $-0.09\pm0.07$ (Mantz). In the Vikhlinin sample $\ln({\rm obs}/{\rm pred})$ has a trend of $-0.31$ per dex of $M_{2500}$ with $p=0.5$ (median obs/pred 0.84, rms$_{\ln}$ 0.225 over twelve clusters) and $-0.35$ with $p=0.46$ (0.76, 0.29). The effect worsens with realistic stellar masses and is independent of the form of $n(\xi)$.

\paragraph{(2) Absolute normalisation.} At $r_{500}$, where $p$ plays no role, obs/pred is 0.95 for Vikhlinin (Chandra) and 1.17 for X-COP (XMM-Newton), a $\sim20\%$ difference that is larger than the radial effect under test; at $r_{200}$ the X-COP value is 1.24. Known cross-calibration differences between the two instruments are of this order and were not modelled. In terms of the ratio tests of Sect.~\ref{sec:frozen} the two samples give $-0.10\pm0.06$ and $+0.03\pm0.04$ for $p=0.5$; their difference of 0.13 in $\ln$ is of the size of the zero-point offset and sets the scale of the cross-sample systematic.

\paragraph{(3) A mass-dependent exponent.} The form $p(\xi)=p_0(\xi/0.4)^q$ with $q>0$ (bootstrap $q=0.37$ [0.31, 0.43], $p_0=0.46$ over Allen and Mantz) removes the trend of tension (1) ($-0.31\to-0.06$, Fig.~\ref{fig:trend}), but predicts a mass trend of $\fbar(r_{200})/\fbar(r_{500})$ of $+0.11$ against $-0.12$ observed and of the within-cluster $r_{2500}/r_{500}$ ratio of $-0.25$ against $-0.07$. It is not supported and is discarded as a hypothesis; the simplest reading remains constant $p\simeq0.5$.

\paragraph{(4) Hydrostatic equilibrium and lensing.} All cluster masses but CLASH are hydrostatic. Hydrostatic masses measure $g_{\rm obs}$ without bias if the gas is in equilibrium in the potential of Eq.~(\ref{eq:K}), but non-thermal pressure ($5.9\%$ at $r_{500}$ and $10.5\%$ at $r_{200}$ in X-COP) and departures from equilibrium were not modelled and the temperature and density profiles predicted by the kernel were not computed. A mass-dependent bias of the sign needed to close tension (1) would require hydrostatic masses to overestimate the true mass at high mass, opposite to the usual expectation from non-thermal pressure. Weighing-the-Giants calibration finds $M_L/M_X=0.96\,(+0.06/-0.09)\pm9\%$ (syst.) at $r_{2500}$, with a mass index $\beta=0.78\,(+0.17/-0.14)$ (assuming $M_L\propto M_X^\beta$), less than $2\sigma$ from 1 (Mantz et al.\ 2016); closing the trend would need $\beta\simeq0.87$. For the 12 clusters common to CLASH and Allen, $M_L/M_X(r_{2500})$ has median 1.21 (weighted mean 1.09, scatter 0.35 in $\ln$), obs/pred is 0.93 with lensing masses and 1.10 with X-ray masses (rms$_{\ln}$ 0.24 and 0.34). The lensing sample is small, high-mass and noisy, the gas was rescaled to the lensing aperture assuming $M_{\rm gas}\propto r^{1.2}$, and noise in $M_L$ enters both axes, so the mass trend itself is not informative there. Lensing calibration therefore reduces but does not decide the matter.

\paragraph{(4b) Sensitivity to a hydrostatic bias.} We set $M_{\rm HSE}=(1-b)M_{\rm true}$ and applied the kernel to $M_{\rm true}$. (a) X-COP, 39 points, $b(w)=b_{500}w^\kappa$ with $\kappa=0$ or 1 and $-0.1\le b_{500}\le0.3$: the best-fit $p$ stays within 0.40--0.49; the rms is 0.016--0.028 dex for the new term, 0.037--0.057 for v4 and 0.110--0.154 without radial term, so the ordering of the three models does not depend on the bias. (b) With measured gas fractions ($\fbar=M_{\rm gas}/M_{\rm true}$) the best-fit $p$ rises with $b$: Vikhlinin (ratio test) 0.53 for $b=0$, 0.56 for $b_{2500}=0.10$--0.15, 0.60 for 0.20--0.25 and 0.63 for 0.30; Allen 0.48, 0.53, 0.57 and 0.60 for $b=-0.1,0,0.1,0.2$. At fixed $p=0.5$ the Vikhlinin ratio test moves from $-0.10\pm0.06$ to $-0.21\pm0.07$ ($b_{2500}=0.15$) and $-0.33\pm0.06$ (0.20), while the v4 law degrades from $-0.61$ to $-1.2$. A bias of 0.17, the value implied by the CLASH--Allen median $M_L/M_X=1.21$, thus corresponds to $p\simeq0.57$, and the Mantz et al.\ calibration ($M_L/M_X=0.96$, $b\simeq-0.04$) to $p\simeq0.52$: both lie inside the sample-to-sample dispersion of Sect.~\ref{sec:frozen}. (c) A positive bias does not remove the mass trend of tension (1): fitting $b=b_0+b_1(\log M_{2500}-14.4)$ to Allen requires $b_1\simeq-0.2$ to $-0.3$ per dex, i.e. $b(10^{15}\,\Msun)\simeq-0.12$ to $-0.21$, hydrostatic masses overestimating the true mass in the most massive clusters. The conclusion on the necessity of the radial term and its ordering against v4 is robust to hydrostatic bias; the amplitude $p$ and the mass trend are not.

\section{Extension to cosmological scales}
\label{sec:cosmo}
\subsection{The kernel applied to linear perturbations}
For a top-hat baryonic perturbation $\delta_b$ of physical radius $R$, $g_{\rm bar}=\tfrac12\Omega_b(a)H^2\delta_bR$ and
\begin{equation}
x=\frac{g_{\rm bar}}{\gdag}=\pi\,\Omega_b(a)\,\delta_b\,\frac{R}{c/H}.
\end{equation}
Over the history of the Universe and for $R=0.1$--100 Mpc comoving, $x=10^{-8}$--$4\times10^{-3}$, always deep in the low-acceleration regime. At $z=1100$ with $\delta_b=10^{-4}$, $K=7282$, 2303, 729 and 231 for $R=0.1,1,10,100$ Mpc, whereas mimicking cold dark matter needs $K=\Omega_m/\Omega_b=6.1$. A toy growth equation $\delta''+(2+d\ln H/d\ln a)\delta'=\tfrac32\Omega_b K(x(\delta))\delta$ from $z=1100$ gives $\delta(z=0)=123$, 127 and 134 for $\delta_i=10^{-5}$, $10^{-4}$, $10^{-3}$ at $R=10$ Mpc comoving (it scales as $1/R$: $1.2\times10^3$ at 1 Mpc, 13--15 at 100 Mpc), against 0.0076, 0.076 and 0.76 in $\Lambda$CDM. The result is nearly independent of the initial amplitude (the force scales as $\delta^{1/2}$), so the primordial amplitude $A_s$ would no longer control the spectrum and the fluctuations would not be linear and Gaussian. The toy neglects photon pressure and baryon--photon coupling before decoupling, which would delay growth, but the excess is $10^3$--$10^5$.

\subsection{Dilemma and damper}
If the kernel acts on cosmological perturbations it overshoots; if it acts only on bound systems, baryons alone grow by a factor 13 between $z=1100$ and today against 760 in $\Lambda$CDM ($\delta=0.013$ instead of 0.76 for $\delta_i=10^{-3}$) and no galaxy forms, and the CMB acoustic peaks cannot be produced without a potential well that does not couple to photons. A damper based on the baryon density contrast,
\begin{equation}
K_{\rm eff}=K_c\,(1-D)+K(x)\,D,\qquad D={\rm expit}\!\left[m\ln\frac{\rho_b/\rho_c}{\Delta_v}\right],\qquad K_c=\frac{\Omega_m}{\Omega_b}\simeq6.12,
\label{eq:damper}
\end{equation}
with $\Delta_v=30$ in $\rho_b/\rho_c$ (total $\Delta\simeq200$) and $m=4$ leaves bound systems unchanged and gives $\delta(z=0)=0.7604$ for $R=1$, 10 and 100 Mpc, identical to $\Lambda$CDM within $10^{-7}$ in the growth rate. This equality holds by construction, since $\rho_b/\rho_c\lesssim0.1$ in the linear regime and $D\to0$: it is consistent but not a test, and $K_c$ is a postulate. A hard cap $K\le K_c$ everywhere is excluded, since at $r_{500}$ the kernel already gives $K\simeq7.5>6.12$ ($\fbar=0.133$ against the cosmic 0.163); a cap would set $\fbar(r_{500})=0.163$ against the observed $\sim0.13$. That clusters sit below the cosmic baryon fraction without feedback is a qualitative consequence of $K>K_c$ in bound systems; we have not tested it against a specific measurement of the baryon depletion.

\subsection{CMB}
Reproducing the acoustic peaks requires a perturbation theory with baryon--photon coupling and a relativistic completion; kernel-type enhancements of the baryon force do not reproduce the third peak and the odd--even peak ratio, and the theories that do (e.g.\ Skordis \& Z\l{}o\'snik 2021) add a field that behaves as pressureless matter. We make no claim about the CMB or large-scale structure.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:disc}
\subsection{What the program claims}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0.1em]
\item In bound systems with symmetric equilibrium the kernel, with $\lambda$, $A$, $\gdag$ fixed on galaxies and groups, describes the baryon fraction across four orders of magnitude in mass with one radial amplitude ($p=0.52\pm0.07$, the error being the dispersion between samples) and one switch in $\xi$ ($\xi_c\simeq0.09$--0.16, width $m\ge3$).
\item The radial term $[\Delta/\Delta_{500}]^p$ replaces the two-parameter exponent modulation, reduces the X-COP rms from 0.037 to 0.016--0.021 dex (in-sample) and, unlike the former, extends inside $r_{500}$ and, with $p$ frozen, to $r_{2500}$ and $r_{200}$ on held-out (not blind) data, with a predicted baryon fraction good to $\sim20\%$ at the amplitude dispersion found.
\item $\gdag\propto H(z)$ is the preferred evolution in clusters and is not excluded in galaxies at $z\simeq1$--2 within current systematics.
\end{enumerate}
This is a phenomenological statement of restricted scope, defensible from the data used and not derived from a Lagrangian.

\subsection{What the program does not claim}
It does not claim that dark matter does not exist, nor that it replaces dark matter in cosmology: the CMB, linear growth and primordial perturbations are untouched, and the damper of Eq.~(\ref{eq:damper}) reproduces $\Lambda$CDM growth by postulate. It does not claim superiority in fit over $\Lambda$CDM haloes, which fit SPARC better and, depending on the unmeasured stellar mass, may fit the groups better. The tensions of Sect.~\ref{sec:tensions} are open.

\subsection{Comparison with competing approaches}
Against MOND (Milgrom 1983) and the RAR (McGaugh et al.\ 2016) the transition scale evolves as $cH(z)/2\pi$ and the exponent depends on the compactness of the equilibrium; against $\Lambda$CDM the kernel is a baryon-only prescription with no per-system halo parameters in the tested regimes, but without cosmology. Observationally the two differ by the redshift evolution of the transition acceleration and by the dependence of the baryon fraction on $\xi$ and radius. Covariant relativistic MOND completions (e.g.\ AeST) address the CMB by adding a field; the kernel does not.

\subsection{Agenda for falsification}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0.1em]
\item \textbf{Radial parameter.} Resolved gas and lensing profiles (X-COP, eROSITA, XXL) should give $p=0.5\pm0.1$ independent of $\xi$ and mass; given the present sample-to-sample dispersion of 0.07, a single sample outside $0.5\pm0.15$ is not decisive, but $|p-0.5|>0.15$ in several independent samples or a significant mass dependence falsifies the scale shift. Apply the archived frozen prescription before looking at the data.
\item \textbf{Evolution of $\gdag$.} Clusters at $z>0.5$ with measured gas fractions (SPT, XMM) and larger samples of $z\simeq1$--2 rotation curves should converge on a common $\alpha$; independent samples preferring $\alpha<0.5$ at $2\sigma$ in clusters, or $\alpha>1$ in galaxies, falsify $\gdag\propto H(z)$.
\item \textbf{The switch.} Systems with $0.09<\xi<0.16$ must show a transition of finite width (for $m=4$, 10\% to 90\% over a factor 3 in $\xi$); galaxies with $\xi>0.16$ should show $s\neq1$, and systems with $\xi<0.09$ should show none. The threshold masses of Sect.~\ref{sec:switch} give the redshift dependence.
\item \textbf{Equilibrium in the kernel potential.} Predict $T(r)$ and $n_{\rm gas}(r)$ for the 12 X-COP and 12 Vikhlinin clusters in the potential of Eq.~(\ref{eq:K}) and compare with the measured profiles, including non-thermal pressure.
\item \textbf{Lensing at low mass.} Per-cluster lensing masses (CCCP, LoCuSS, WtG) below $M_{2500}\simeq2\times10^{14}\,\Msun$ to decide the mass trend of Sect.~\ref{sec:tensions}; measured $M_*(<r_{2500})$ in groups to fix $\varphi$.
\item \textbf{Systems not tested here.} El Gordo ($\xi\simeq0.73$) is an active merger and violates the equilibrium premise; ATLAS-3D/SLUGGS early-type galaxies and DESI rotation curves remain open from Paper~I.
\item \textbf{Covariant formulation.} Without an action the program has no predictions for gravitational waves, the CMB or strong fields.
\end{enumerate}

%--------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusions}
The kernel of Paper~I, extended with a single-amplitude radial term, describes bound systems from galaxies to clusters better than before; it remains a phenomenological map, not a cosmological theory.

\paragraph{Established.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0.1em]
\item The radial term is necessary: without it the kernel mispredicts the baryon fraction at $r_{2500}$ by a factor 3--4 and at $r_{200}$ by a factor 2.4.
\item $s=[\Delta/\Delta_{500}]^p$ with $p\simeq0.45$--0.5 describes it with one amplitude: X-COP rms 0.126 $\to$ 0.016--0.021 dex (v4 law: 0.037; in-sample), and with $p=0.5$ frozen the ratio tests give $\ln({\rm obs/pred})=-0.10\pm0.06$ at $r_{2500}$ (Vikhlinin; v4: $-0.61\pm0.21$) and $+0.03\pm0.04$ at $r_{200}$ (Ettori; v4: $+0.02\pm0.06$).
\item The v4 law $w^{-\alpha(\xi)}$ is adequate for $w\gtrsim0.7$ in X-COP but fails inside $r_{500}$ ($n>1$) and does not meet the v4 criterion of 0.10 dex at $r_{2500}$.
\item $\gdag\propto H(z)$ is preferred by Allen and Mantz gas fractions; a frozen $\gdag$ costs $\Delta\chi^2=57$ and 48.
\item A threshold in $\xi$ ($\xi_c=0.09$--0.16, width $m\ge3$) connects galaxies, groups and clusters; a smooth power law $p(\xi)$ is disfavoured by $\Delta(-2\ln L)\simeq50$, and the threshold mass scales with redshift by 0.57 ($z=1$) and 0.34 ($z=2$).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Not established.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0.1em]
\item The amplitude $p$ is not a constant at the current precision: the best-fit values range from 0.45 to 0.60 between samples (mean 0.52, standard deviation 0.07), a systematic of order 20\% in the predicted baryon fraction.
\item The sharpness and location of the galaxy--group switch are loosely constrained, and the group amplitude depends on an unmeasured stellar mass.
\item The baryon fraction at $r_{2500}$ rises with mass more than observed ($-0.31$ per dex in obs/pred); lensing calibration shifts it by $\sim1\sigma$.
\item The absolute normalisation differs by $\sim20\%$ between the Chandra and XMM samples (0.95 against 1.17 at $r_{500}$).
\item A mass-dependent $p(\xi)\propto\xi^{0.37}$ closes the former but predicts the wrong sign of the $r_{500}\to r_{200}$ trend, and is discarded.
\item Median KMOS$^{\rm 3D}$ galaxies prefer $\gdag\propto E^{0.5\text{--}0.75}$ against $\alpha\gtrsim1$ in clusters, within the $\sim10\%$ systematics of the former.
\item Applied to linear perturbations the kernel boosts gravity $10^2$--$10^3$ times; the damper recovers $\Lambda$CDM growth only through the postulated plateau $K_c=\Omega_m/\Omega_b$; no CMB statement is made.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Position relative to $\Lambda$CDM.} In clusters the kernel is competitive (X-COP AIC $-301$ to $-321$ against $-227$, in-sample; margin 34--66 after charging $p$, $\xi_c$, $m$, $A$); in galaxies it is parsimonious (zero parameters) but fits worse than haloes (0.142 against 0.087 or 0.059 dex); in groups the kernel and $\Lambda$CDM are indistinguishable with Kravtsov stellar masses and $\Lambda$CDM is better with lower ones; in cosmology the kernel makes no competitive claim. The defensible claim is an empirical map of bound systems that any covariant theory must reproduce in the weak-field stationary limit.

\paragraph{Practical value of parsimony.} The model has no per-system free parameter: given the baryonic profile and the equilibrium geometry of a system, $\Keff$ returns $g_{\rm obs}$ (or the baryon fraction) from five global numbers, $\lambda$ and $\gdag$ fixed on galaxies and $A$, $p$ and the switch fixed on groups and clusters, all frozen in the archived prescription. Three practical consequences follow, within the accuracy documented above. (i) \emph{Forecasting without a halo fit.} A rotation curve, a baryon fraction at $r_{2500}$ or the threshold mass at which a system leaves the galactic regime can be stated before the data are taken, with an error budget (0.14 dex in galaxies, $\sim20\%$ in the cluster baryon fraction), whereas an NFW description needs one or two free parameters per galaxy (141--282 for the SPARC sample) or one mass per cluster, and can absorb a mismatch that the kernel cannot. (ii) \emph{Cheap and auditable computation.} Each prediction is a single root-finding with no sampling, so it can be applied to large surveys or to sparse and high-redshift data where halo parameters are unconstrained, and any discrepancy is attributable to one of a handful of numbers rather than to a degenerate fit. (iii) \emph{Falsifiability.} The same scarcity of parameters is what exposed the tensions of Sect.~\ref{sec:tensions} and the dispersion of $p$ in Sect.~\ref{sec:frozen}; each further sample either confirms the frozen values or breaks them. Parsimony is a virtue only while the accuracy holds: the kernel fits galaxies worse than haloes (0.142 against 0.087 dex), the amplitude $p$ carries a $\sim20\%$ systematic, and it needs a measured baryonic profile as input. Its practical role is therefore a first-order, reproducible estimator of dynamical masses in bound systems, and a sharp test for competing descriptions, not a replacement of halo fitting where precision is required.
