Introduction

The kernel proposed in the first paper of this program (v4, hereafter Paper I) maps the baryonic
acceleration g_(bar) of a bound system to its observed acceleration, g_(obs) = K_(eff) g_(bar),
using a transition scale g^(†)(z) = cH(z)/2π and an exponent n that depends on the equilibrium
geometry through the compactness ξ = g₅₀₀/g^(†)(z). Paper I tested it on SPARC disk galaxies, 23
groups, 13 X-COP clusters and 23 CLASH clusters and listed its own limitations. Four of them are the
subject of this work.

1.  The radial law was semi-predictive. The factor w^(−α(ξ)), with α(ξ) = 0.355(1−e^(−ξ/0.052)),
    used two parameters derived earlier from a subset of eight X-COP clusters, and the 39-point test
    of Paper I partially overlaps that calibration. Paper I lists validation on resolved profiles,
    with a falsification criterion of 0.10 dex, as an open item.

2.  The evidence for g^(†) ∝ H(z) rested on CLASH gas fractions estimated from a scaling relation.
    Paper I itself discusses whether part of the residual correlation is algebraic.

3.  Groups were 23 and no variable switched the radial term between galaxies and clusters.

4.  No test at high redshift and no treatment of cosmological scales were given; the extension was
    declared outside the scope.

We (i) replace the radial law by a one-parameter scale shift and compare it with the v4 law on the
same and on independent data (Sect. 4); (ii) test g^(†)(z) with measured gas fractions and with
z ≃ 1–2 galaxies (Sect. 5); (iii) introduce a step in ξ connecting galaxies to groups and clusters
and derive its redshift dependence (Sect. 6); (iv) compare with ΛCDM (Sect. 7); (v) document the
tensions (Sect. 8); and (vi) examine what happens when the kernel is applied to cosmological
perturbations and how a density-based damper behaves (Sect. 9). Section 10 states what the program
does and does not claim, and lists falsifiable tests.

The kernel and its radial extension

Definition

For a bound, spherically or axially symmetric system in equilibrium,

    K_(eff)= 1+(λ e^(-x/s))/((1-e^(-x/s))^n), x=(g_(bar))/(g^†(z)), g^†(z)=(cH(z))/2π,

with λ = 0.999, H₀ = 70 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹, Ω_(m) = 0.3, and the exponent law of Paper I,

    n(ξ)=1/2+A ξ, A=0.4068, ξ=g₅₀₀/(g^†(z))=(2π G M₅₀₀)/(c H(z) R₅₀₀²).

For s = 1 and ξ → 0 this is the disk-galaxy kernel (n = 1/2) with the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation
v_(f)⁴ = λ²g^(†)GM_(b) as a structural consequence. The compactness ξ scales as (MH)^(1/3).

Radial term

Paper I modulated the exponent, n → n(ξ) w^(−α(ξ)) with w = r/R₅₀₀. Here the exponent is kept at its
w = 1 value and the radial dependence enters through the scale s,

    s=[(Δ(r))/Δ₅₀₀]^(p Θ(ξ-ξ_c)), (Δ(r))/Δ₅₀₀=(ρ(<r))/(500 ρ_c(z))=(M(<r))/M₅₀₀(R₅₀₀/r)³.

By definition Δ/Δ₅₀₀ = 1 at r₅₀₀, 5 at r₂₅₀₀ and 0.4 at r₂₀₀, so p is the only radial parameter and
the w = 1 calibration of Paper I is untouched. Θ is a step in ξ, implemented as
Θ = [1+(ξ_(c)/ξ)^(m)]⁻¹ with m ≥ 32, and ξ_(c) is bracketed by the data (Sect. 6). Systems with
ξ < ξ_(c) have s = 1.

Self-consistent baryon fraction

In a system with total enclosed mass M(<r) and baryonic fraction f = M_(bar)/M, g_(obs) = GM/r² and
x = f g_(obs)/g^(†); the identity K = g_(obs)/g_(bar) = 1/f gives the equation
f K(f g_(obs)/g^(†)) = 1, solved for f. The prediction is a baryon fraction, which is compared with
the observed gas plus stellar fraction. Because the observable depends on the measured total mass,
hydrostatic masses are interpreted as the effective dynamical mass g_(obs)r²/G; they are unbiased
with respect to the gravity law if the gas is in equilibrium in the potential of Eq. ([eq:K])
(Sect. 8).

Statistics.

Residuals are in log₁₀ (dex) unless stated as ln ; model comparison uses  − 2ln L = ∑Nln (rms²) with
AIC  = ∑Nln (rms²) + 2k, counting only the parameters added to the fixed λ, A, g^(†). When the
observable measures only the redshift or radial shape (Mantz shells, Allen cumulative fractions) the
normalisation is a free scalar. Cluster stellar masses follow Kravtsov et al. (2018) (the relation
of Gonzalez et al. 2013 was used only as an alternative in cross-checks),
log M_(*,tot) = 12.71 + 0.59(logM₅₀₀−14.5) (0.11 dex scatter, M₅₀₀ = 4.6 × 10¹³–1.6 × 10¹⁵ M_(⊙)),
with a fraction φ of it inside r₂₅₀₀ (φ = 0.7, 1.0 inside r₅₀₀). A hard-coded stellar fraction of 9%
of the gas is used where noted. The radial parameter p and φ are the only quantities adjusted
outside the calibration of Paper I; φ has not been measured and is varied (0.5–0.7).

Independent data

Vikhlinin et al. (2006), r₂₅₀₀.

For the ten clusters with cumulative f_(gas) at both radii (ξ = 0.23–0.59,
M₅₀₀ = 7.4 × 10¹³–1.1 × 10¹⁵ M_(⊙), w = r₂₅₀₀/r₅₀₀ = 0.39–0.48) the control at r₅₀₀, where the
radial term plays no role, gives obs/pred  = 0.95 (rms 0.13 in ln ). At r₂₅₀₀ without a radial term
obs/pred is 0.26 (rms_(ln) 1.35, predicted f_(bar)(r₂₅₀₀)/f_(bar)(r₅₀₀) = 3.37); with the v4 law
0.45 (rms_(ln) 0.90, ratio 1.95, and one of the ten clusters has no solution because n > 1 inside
w ≃ 0.45); with the scale shift 0.84 (rms_(ln) 0.23, ratio 1.00). The observed ratio is 0.90. The v4
law, which scales the exponent as w^(−α), drives n beyond 1 at w < 1 for the highest ξ, where the
kernel no longer has a finite baryon fraction; the scale shift cannot do this because it multiplies
x rather than the exponent. At the criterion proposed in Paper I (0.10 dex), the v4 law with its
published α(ξ) fails on this sample (we did not re-optimise α).

X-COP out to r₂₀₀.

Ettori et al. (2019, Table 2) give f_(gas) and M_(HSE) at r₅₀₀ and r₂₀₀ for 12 clusters. After
adding stars (Kravtsov scaling, φ = 1) the ratio f_(bar)(r₂₀₀)/f_(bar)(r₅₀₀) is observed to be 1.03
(median); the scale shift with p = 0.5 predicts 0.98 (all 12) or 0.97 (the nine clusters left after
excluding the mergers A2319, A3266 and ZW1215), against 0.97 for the v4 law (rms of ln [obs/pred
ratio] 0.157 against 0.120 for the new term) and 0.42 without a radial term. The mass trend of this
ratio is  − 0.12 per dex observed and  − 0.11 predicted. The mass-dependent exponent p(ξ) ∝ ξ^(0.37)
(Sect. 8) predicts  + 0.11 and is disfavoured. At r₅₀₀ itself, the X-COP sample has obs/pred  = 1.17
(rms 0.19 in ln ) while the Vikhlinin sample gives 0.95 (Sect. 8).

Gain of the radial term.

Without the radial term the kernel overestimates the baryon fraction at r₂₅₀₀ by a factor 3–4
(obs/pred 0.27, rms_(ln) 1.27 over 12 clusters) and underestimates it at r₂₀₀ by a factor 2.4 (ratio
0.42 against 1.03). With p = 0.5 the rms falls to 0.225 at r₂₅₀₀ and 0.12 at r₂₀₀. In galaxies
ξ < ξ_(c), so s = 1 and the term is inactive: the gain belongs to groups and clusters. The test
shows that the term is necessary and that [Δ/Δ₅₀₀]^(p) describes it with one parameter; it does not
show that this is the only possible form, and p ≃ 0.5 was calibrated on samples that partially
overlap (A1795 and A2029 are in both Vikhlinin and X-COP).

Evolution of g^(†)

We parametrise g^(†)(z) = g^(†)(0) E(z)^(α), with α = 1 the prediction g^(†) = cH/2π and α = 0 a
frozen scale. In clusters α enters both x and ξ, so a change of α moves the exponent as well as the
transition.

scaling relations with 0.2 dex error and no atomic gas, pressure-support corrections of 0.1–0.2 dex,
selection that changes the stellar mass across bins) are of order 10% or more, which they are; with
three bins we cannot decide whether α depends on system scale. The dark matter fractions inferred in
z ≃ 2 galaxies are lower than at z = 0 (Übler et al. 2017; Genzel et al. 2017), whereas a larger
g^(†) at the same baryonic acceleration produces a larger K; this is the same tension seen from
another side and has not been tested quantitatively here.

Connecting galaxies to clusters: the ξ switch

The radial term cannot be applied to galaxies: in SPARC an exploratory s = (Δ/Δ_(out))^(p), with Δ
taken from the rotation curve itself, degrades the rms from 0.148 to 0.158, 0.235 and 0.468 dex for
p = 0.25, 0.5 and 1, and the global fit favours p ≃ 0.09. A switch based on the local density does
not separate the regimes, because inner cluster radii reach galactic densities; the discriminator
must be a property of the system. The compactness ξ is such a property: SPARC haloes have
ξ ≃ 0.004–0.094 (under two halo mass assumptions), the 43 groups ξ = 0.136–0.29, X-COP 0.34–0.54 and
the Allen/Mantz clusters 0.40–0.85. A step Θ(ξ−ξ_(c)) therefore needs 0.09 ≲ ξ_(c) ≲ 0.14, a bracket
set by the absence of systems in 0.09 < ξ < 0.136. A saturating switch such as 1 − e^(−ξ/0.052), the
form inherited from α(ξ) in Paper I, turns the term on partially at galactic ξ and was found
incompatible with the SPARC residuals in our test.

Because ξ ∝ (M₅₀₀H)^(1/3) the switch predicts a redshift-dependent threshold mass,

  M₅₀₀^(thr) [M_(⊙)]      z = 0        z = 1        z = 2
  -------------------- ------------ ------------ ------------
  ξ_(c) = 0.09          4.2 × 10¹²   2.4 × 10¹²   1.4 × 10¹²
  ξ_(c) = 0.14          1.6 × 10¹³    9 × 10¹²    5.4 × 10¹²

and, for the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation, a zero-point shift Δlog v = 0.25log₁₀E(z) ( + 0.061 dex
at z = 1,  + 0.118 at z = 2). The step itself is the simplest function that connects the regimes;
its sharpness is unconstrained.

ΛCDM fits SPARC rotation curves better (0.087 or 0.059 against 0.142 dex), at the cost of one or two
free parameters per galaxy; the kernel has none, and equals the RAR in quality (0.148 against
0.144). In groups ΛCDM with a c(M) prior and no free parameter is better at p = 0.5 with 9% stars;
the old-stellar-population value p = 0.8 gives 0.056. In X-COP the radial term wins by AIC with a
margin of 74 to 94 over NFW + Duffy, which has 12 more parameters, but this is within the
calibration sample and with the Paper I baryon fraction. A positive offset of  + 0.15 dex appears in
SPARC galaxies with V_(max) < 70 km s⁻¹ (312 points, rms 0.25), which we find not to depend on
M_(bar) and interpret as a selection effect of grouping by the observed maximum velocity. We
therefore describe the kernel as competitive in clusters and parsimonious in galaxies, not as
superior in fit.

Tensions and tests that did not close

(1) Baryon fraction against mass at r₂₅₀₀.

The kernel predicts f_(bar)(r₂₅₀₀) rising with M₂₅₀₀ by  + 0.35 per dex, against a measured mass
slope of the observed fraction of  − 0.04 ± 0.09 (Allen) and  − 0.09 ± 0.07 (Mantz). In the
Vikhlinin sample ln (obs/pred) has a trend of  − 0.31 per dex of M₂₅₀₀ with p = 0.5 (median obs/pred
0.84, rms_(ln) 0.225 over twelve clusters) and  − 0.35 with p = 0.46 (0.76, 0.29). The effect
worsens with realistic stellar masses and is independent of the form of n(ξ).

(2) Absolute normalisation.

At r₅₀₀, where p plays no role, obs/pred is 0.95 for Vikhlinin (Chandra) and 1.17 for X-COP
(XMM-Newton), a  ∼ 20% difference that is larger than the radial effect under test; at r₂₀₀ the
X-COP value is 1.24. Known cross-calibration differences between the two instruments are of this
order and were not modelled.

(3) A mass-dependent exponent.

The form p(ξ) = p₀(ξ/0.4)^(q) with q > 0 (bootstrap q = 0.37 [0.31, 0.43], p₀ = 0.46 over Allen and
Mantz) removes the trend of tension (1) ( − 0.31 →  − 0.06, Fig. 3), but predicts a mass trend of
f_(bar)(r₂₀₀)/f_(bar)(r₅₀₀) of  + 0.11 against  − 0.12 observed and of the within-cluster r₂₅₀₀/r₅₀₀
ratio of  − 0.25 against  − 0.07. It is not supported and is discarded as a hypothesis; the simplest
reading remains constant p ≃ 0.5.

(4) Hydrostatic equilibrium and lensing.

All cluster masses but CLASH are hydrostatic. Hydrostatic masses measure g_(obs) without bias if the
gas is in equilibrium in the potential of Eq. ([eq:K]), but non-thermal pressure (5.9% at r₅₀₀ and
10.5% at r₂₀₀ in X-COP) and departures from equilibrium were not modelled and the temperature and
density profiles predicted by the kernel were not computed. A mass-dependent bias of the sign needed
to close tension (1) would require hydrostatic masses to overestimate the true mass at high mass,
opposite to the usual expectation from non-thermal pressure. Weighing-the-Giants calibration finds
M_(L)/M_(X) = 0.96 (+0.06/−0.09) ± 9% (syst.) at r₂₅₀₀, with a mass index β = 0.78 (+0.17/−0.14)
(assuming M_(L) ∝ M_(X)^(β)), less than 2σ from 1 (Mantz et al. 2016); closing the trend would need
β ≃ 0.87. For the 12 clusters common to CLASH and Allen, M_(L)/M_(X)(r₂₅₀₀) has median 1.21
(weighted mean 1.09, scatter 0.35 in ln ), obs/pred is 0.93 with lensing masses and 1.10 with X-ray
masses (rms_(ln) 0.24 and 0.34). The lensing sample is small, high-mass and noisy, the gas was
rescaled to the lensing aperture assuming M_(gas) ∝ r^(1.2), and noise in M_(L) enters both axes, so
the mass trend itself is not informative there. Lensing calibration therefore reduces but does not
decide the matter.

Sect. 5.

Extension to cosmological scales

The kernel applied to linear perturbations

For a top-hat baryonic perturbation δ_(b) of physical radius R,
g_(bar)=1/2Ω_b(a)H²δ_bR and

    x=(g_(bar))/(g^†)=π Ω_b(a) δ_b R/(c/H).

Over the history of the Universe and for R = 0.1–100 Mpc comoving, x = 10⁻⁸–4 × 10⁻³, always deep in
the low-acceleration regime. At z = 1100 with δ_(b) = 10⁻⁴, K = 7282, 2303, 729 and 231 for
R = 0.1, 1, 10, 100 Mpc, whereas mimicking cold dark matter needs K = Ω_(m)/Ω_(b) = 6.1. A toy
growth equation δ''+(2+dln H/dln a)δ'=3/2Ω_b K(x(δ))δ from
z = 1100 gives δ(z=0) = 123, 127 and 134 for δ_(i) = 10⁻⁵, 10⁻⁴, 10⁻³ at R = 10 Mpc comoving (it
scales as 1/R: 1.2 × 10³ at 1 Mpc, 13–15 at 100 Mpc), against 0.0076, 0.076 and 0.76 in ΛCDM. The
result is nearly independent of the initial amplitude (the force scales as δ^(1/2)), so the
primordial amplitude A_(s) would no longer control the spectrum and the fluctuations would not be
linear and Gaussian. The toy neglects photon pressure and baryon–photon coupling before decoupling,
which would delay growth, but the excess is 10³–10⁵.

Dilemma and damper

If the kernel acts on cosmological perturbations it overshoots; if it acts only on bound systems,
baryons alone grow by a factor 13 between z = 1100 and today against 760 in ΛCDM (δ = 0.013 instead
of 0.76 for δ_(i) = 10⁻³) and no galaxy forms, and the CMB acoustic peaks cannot be produced without
a potential well that does not couple to photons. A damper based on the baryon density contrast,

    K_(eff)=K_c (1-D)+K(x) D, D=expit[mln(ρ_b/ρ_c)/Δ_v], K_c=Ω_m/Ω_b≃6.12,

with Δ_(v) = 30 in ρ_(b)/ρ_(c) (total Δ ≃ 200) and m = 4 leaves bound systems unchanged and gives
δ(z=0) = 0.7604 for R = 1, 10 and 100 Mpc, identical to ΛCDM within 10⁻⁷ in the growth rate. This
equality holds by construction, since ρ_(b)/ρ_(c) ≲ 0.1 in the linear regime and D → 0: it is
consistent but not a test, and K_(c) is a postulate. A hard cap K ≤ K_(c) everywhere is excluded,
since at r₅₀₀ the kernel already gives K ≃ 7.5 > 6.12 (f_(bar) = 0.133 against the cosmic 0.163); a
cap would set f_(bar)(r₅₀₀) = 0.163 against the observed  ∼ 0.13. That clusters sit below the cosmic
baryon fraction without feedback is a qualitative consequence of K > K_(c) in bound systems; we have
not tested it against a specific measurement of the baryon depletion.

CMB

Reproducing the acoustic peaks requires a perturbation theory with baryon–photon coupling and a
relativistic completion; kernel-type enhancements of the baryon force do not reproduce the third
peak and the odd–even peak ratio, and the theories that do (e.g. Skordis & Złośnik 2021) add a field
that behaves as pressureless matter. We make no claim about the CMB or large-scale structure.

Discussion

What the program claims

1.  In bound systems with symmetric equilibrium the kernel, with λ, A, g^(†) fixed on galaxies and
    groups, describes the baryon fraction across four orders of magnitude in mass with one radial
    parameter (p ≃ 0.45–0.5) and one switch (ξ_(c) ≃ 0.09–0.14).

2.  The radial term [Δ/Δ₅₀₀]^(p) replaces the two-parameter exponent modulation, reduces the X-COP
    rms from 0.037 to 0.016–0.021 dex and, unlike the former, extends to r₂₅₀₀ and to r₂₀₀ on
    independent data.

3.  g^(†) ∝ H(z) is the preferred evolution in clusters and is not excluded in galaxies at z ≃ 1–2
    within current systematics.

This is a phenomenological statement of restricted scope, defensible from the data used and not
derived from a Lagrangian.

What the program does not claim

It does not claim that dark matter does not exist, nor that it replaces dark matter in cosmology:
the CMB, linear growth and primordial perturbations are untouched, and the damper of
Eq. ([eq:damper]) reproduces ΛCDM growth by postulate. It does not claim superiority in fit over
ΛCDM haloes, which fit SPARC and, with the stellar assumption used, the groups better. The tensions
of Sect. 8 are open.

Comparison with competing approaches

Against MOND (Milgrom 1983) and the RAR (McGaugh et al. 2016) the transition scale evolves as
cH(z)/2π and the exponent depends on the compactness of the equilibrium; against ΛCDM the kernel is
a baryon-only prescription with no per-system halo parameters in the tested regimes, but without
cosmology. Observationally the two differ by the redshift evolution of the transition acceleration
and by the dependence of the baryon fraction on ξ and radius. Covariant relativistic MOND
completions (e.g. AeST) address the CMB by adding a field; the kernel does not.

Agenda for falsification

1.  Radial parameter. Resolved gas and lensing profiles (X-COP, eROSITA, XXL) should give
    p = 0.5 ± 0.1 independent of ξ and mass; |p−0.5| > 0.15 or a significant mass dependence
    falsifies the scale shift.

2.  Evolution of g^(†). Clusters at z > 0.5 with measured gas fractions (SPT, XMM) and larger
    samples of z ≃ 1–2 rotation curves should converge on a common α; independent samples preferring
    α < 0.5 at 2σ in clusters, or α > 1 in galaxies, falsify g^(†) ∝ H(z).

3.  The switch. Systems with 0.09 < ξ < 0.14 must show a transition; galaxies with ξ > 0.14 should
    show s ≠ 1, and systems with ξ < 0.09 should show none. The threshold masses of Sect. 6 give the
    redshift dependence.

4.  Equilibrium in the kernel potential. Predict T(r) and n_(gas)(r) for the 12 X-COP and 12
    Vikhlinin clusters in the potential of Eq. ([eq:K]) and compare with the measured profiles,
    including non-thermal pressure.

5.  Lensing at low mass. Per-cluster lensing masses (CCCP, LoCuSS, WtG) below M₂₅₀₀ ≃ 2 × 10¹⁴ M_(⊙)
    to decide the mass trend of Sect. 8; measured M_(*)(<r₂₅₀₀) in groups to fix φ.

6.  Systems not tested here. El Gordo (ξ ≃ 0.73) is an active merger and violates the equilibrium
    premise; ATLAS-3D/SLUGGS early-type galaxies and DESI rotation curves remain open from Paper I.

7.  Covariant formulation. Without an action the program has no predictions for gravitational waves,
    the CMB or strong fields.

Conclusions

The kernel of Paper I, extended with a one-parameter radial term, describes bound systems from
galaxies to clusters better than before; it remains a phenomenological map, not a cosmological
theory.

Established.

1.  The radial term is necessary: without it the kernel mispredicts the baryon fraction at r₂₅₀₀ by
    a factor 3–4 and at r₂₀₀ by a factor 2.4.

2.  s = [Δ/Δ₅₀₀]^(p) with p ≃ 0.45–0.5 describes it with one parameter: X-COP rms 0.126 →
    0.016–0.021 dex (v4 law: 0.037), f_(bar)(r₂₅₀₀)/f_(bar)(r₅₀₀) predicted 1.00 against 0.90
    observed, f_(bar)(r₂₀₀)/f_(bar)(r₅₀₀) 0.97 against 1.03.

3.  The v4 law w^(−α(ξ)) is adequate for w ≳ 0.7 in X-COP but fails inside r₅₀₀ (n > 1) and does not
    meet the v4 criterion of 0.10 dex at r₂₅₀₀.

4.  g^(†) ∝ H(z) is preferred by Allen and Mantz gas fractions; a frozen g^(†) costs Δχ² = 57 and
    48.

5.  A step in ξ at ξ_(c) = 0.09–0.14 connects galaxies, groups and clusters, with redshift-dependent
    threshold masses (4 × 10¹² to 1.4 × 10¹² M_(⊙) from z = 0 to 2 for ξ_(c) = 0.09).

Not established.

1.  The baryon fraction at r₂₅₀₀ rises with mass more than observed ( − 0.31 per dex in obs/pred);
    lensing calibration shifts it by  ∼ 1σ.

2.  The absolute normalisation differs by  ∼ 20% between the Chandra and XMM samples (0.95 against
    1.17 at r₅₀₀).

3.  A mass-dependent p(ξ) ∝ ξ^(0.37) closes the former but predicts the wrong sign of the
    r₅₀₀ → r₂₀₀ trend, and is discarded.

4.  Median KMOS^(3D) galaxies prefer g^(†) ∝ E^(0.5–0.75) against α ≳ 1 in clusters, within the
     ∼ 10% systematics of the former.

5.  Applied to linear perturbations the kernel boosts gravity 10²–10³ times; the damper recovers
    ΛCDM growth only through the postulated plateau K_(c) = Ω_(m)/Ω_(b); no CMB statement is made.

Position relative to ΛCDM.

In clusters the kernel is competitive (X-COP AIC  − 301 to  − 321 against  − 227, in-sample); in
galaxies it is parsimonious (zero parameters) but fits worse than haloes (0.142 against 0.087 or
0.059 dex); in groups ΛCDM is better for p = 0.5 with the stellar assumption used; in cosmology the
kernel makes no competitive claim. The defensible claim is an empirical map of bound systems that
any covariant theory must reproduce in the weak-field stationary limit.
