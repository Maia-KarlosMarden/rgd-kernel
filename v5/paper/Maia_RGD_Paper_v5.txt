Introduction

The kernel proposed in the first paper of this program (v4, hereafter Paper I) maps the baryonic
acceleration g_(bar) of a bound system to its observed acceleration, g_(obs) = K_(eff) g_(bar),
using a transition scale g^(†)(z) = cH(z)/2π and an exponent n that depends on the equilibrium
geometry through the compactness ξ = g₅₀₀/g^(†)(z). Paper I tested it on SPARC disk galaxies, 23
groups, 13 X-COP clusters and 23 CLASH clusters and listed its own limitations. Four of them are the
subject of this work.

1.  The radial law was semi-predictive. The factor w^(−α(ξ)), with α(ξ) = 0.355(1−e^(−ξ/0.052)),
    used two parameters derived earlier from a subset of eight X-COP clusters, and the 39-point test
    of Paper I partially overlaps that calibration. Paper I lists validation on resolved profiles,
    with a falsification criterion of 0.10 dex, as an open item.

2.  The evidence for g^(†) ∝ H(z) rested on CLASH gas fractions estimated from a scaling relation.
    Paper I itself discusses whether part of the residual correlation is algebraic.

3.  Groups were 23 and no variable switched the radial term between galaxies and clusters.

4.  No test at high redshift and no treatment of cosmological scales were given; the extension was
    declared outside the scope.

We (i) replace the radial law by a scale shift with a single amplitude and compare it with the v4
law on the same data and, with the amplitude frozen, on other samples (Sect. 4); (ii) test g^(†)(z)
with measured gas fractions and with z ≃ 1–2 galaxies (Sect. 5); (iii) introduce a switch in ξ
connecting galaxies to groups and clusters, test whether it must be a sharp step, and derive its
redshift dependence (Sect. 6); (iv) compare with ΛCDM (Sect. 7); (v) document the tensions
(Sect. 8); and (vi) examine what happens when the kernel is applied to cosmological perturbations
and how a density-based damper behaves (Sect. 9). Section 10 states what the program does and does
not claim, and lists falsifiable tests.

The kernel and its radial extension

Definition

For a bound, spherically or axially symmetric system in equilibrium,

    K_(eff)= 1+(λ e^(-x/s))/((1-e^(-x/s))^n), x=(g_(bar))/(g^†(z)), g^†(z)=(cH(z))/2π,

with λ = 0.999, H₀=70 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹, Ω_(m) = 0.3, and the exponent law of Paper I,

    n(ξ)=1/2+A ξ, A=0.4068, ξ=g₅₀₀/(g^†(z))=(2π G M₅₀₀)/(c H(z) R₅₀₀²).

For s = 1 and ξ → 0 this is the disk-galaxy kernel (n = 1/2) with the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation
v_(f)⁴ = λ²g^(†)GM_(b) as a structural consequence. The compactness ξ scales as (MH)^(1/3).

Radial term

Paper I modulated the exponent, n → n(ξ) w^(−α(ξ)) with w = r/R₅₀₀. Here the exponent is kept at its
w = 1 value and the radial dependence enters through the scale s,

    s=[(Δ(r))/Δ₅₀₀]^(p Θ(ξ)), (Δ(r))/Δ₅₀₀=(ρ(<r))/(500 ρ_c(z))=(M(<r))/M₅₀₀(R₅₀₀/r)³.

By definition Δ/Δ₅₀₀ = 1 at r₅₀₀, 5 at r₂₅₀₀ and 0.4 at r₂₀₀, so p is the only radial amplitude and
the w = 1 calibration of Paper I is untouched. Θ(ξ) = [1+(ξ_(c)/ξ)^(m)]⁻¹ is a logistic switch in
ln ξ. Sect. 6 shows that the data constrain neither its sharpness nor its location tightly (m ≥ 3,
0.09 ≲ ξ_(c) ≲ 0.16); the fiducial values are m = 4, ξ_(c) = 0.11. For clusters (ξ ≥ 0.34) Θ ≥ 0.98
for every acceptable choice, so the cluster results below do not depend on it; systems with
ξ ≪ ξ_(c) have s = 1.

Self-consistent baryon fraction

In a system with total enclosed mass M(<r) and baryonic fraction f = M_(bar)/M, g_(obs) = GM/r² and
x = f g_(obs)/g^(†); the identity K = g_(obs)/g_(bar) = 1/f gives the equation
f K(f g_(obs)/g^(†)) = 1, solved for f. The prediction is a baryon fraction, which is compared with
the observed gas plus stellar fraction. Because the observable depends on the measured total mass,
hydrostatic masses are interpreted as the effective dynamical mass g_(obs)r²/G; they are unbiased
with respect to the gravity law if the gas is in equilibrium in the potential of Eq. (1)
(Sect. 8).

Statistics.

Residuals are in log₁₀ (dex) unless stated as ln ; model comparison uses  − 2ln L = ∑Nln (rms²) with
AIC  = ∑Nln (rms²) + 2k. Points at different radii of one cluster, and cumulative profiles, are
strongly correlated, so we also give results with one independent unit per cluster (N_(eff) = 13 for
X-COP; 141 galaxies and 43 groups in joint fits), and we count k in two ways: only the parameters
added in the comparison, and in addition p, ξ_(c), m and A (Sect. 7). Intervals on rms values,
paired differences and best-fit p are cluster bootstraps (2000–4000 resamplings of the clusters).
When the observable measures only the redshift or radial shape (Mantz shells, Allen cumulative
fractions in the α scan) the normalisation is a free scalar. Cluster stellar masses follow Kravtsov
et al. (2018) (the relation of Gonzalez et al. 2013 was used only as an alternative in
cross-checks), log M_(*,tot)=12.71+0.59(log M₅₀₀-14.5) (0.11 dex scatter,
M₅₀₀ = 4.6 × 10¹³–1.6 × 10¹⁵ M_(⊙)), with a fraction φ of it inside r₂₅₀₀ (φ = 0.7, 1.0 inside
r₅₀₀). A stellar fraction of 9% of the gas, used in the first draft for groups, is kept as a
sensitivity case. φ has not been measured and is varied (0.5–1).

Uncertainties.

Resampling the 13 clusters (4000 draws) gives rms 0.126 [0.111, 0.140] without radial term, 0.037
[0.026, 0.047] for the v4 law, 0.021 [0.014, 0.027] for p = 0.5 and 0.016 [0.012, 0.019] for the
fitted p = 0.45 [0.40, 0.50] (95% intervals). The paired difference rms(v4)−rms(p = 0.5) is  + 0.016
[0.007, 0.024] dex and is positive in every draw. Counting one independent unit per cluster
(N_(eff) = 13) the AIC is  − 54,  − 82,  − 101 and  − 105 for no radial term, v4, p = 0.5 and fitted
p (against  − 162,  − 253,  − 301,  − 321 with N = 39): the ordering is unchanged and the margin of
the new term over v4 is 19–23. Charging p (and ξ_(c), m, A as in Sect. 7) lowers these margins by at
most 8.

Independent data

Vikhlinin et al. (2006), r₂₅₀₀.

For the ten clusters with cumulative f_(gas) at both radii (ξ = 0.23–0.59,
M₅₀₀ = 7.4 × 10¹³–1.1 × 10¹⁵ M_(⊙), w = r₂₅₀₀/r₅₀₀ = 0.39–0.48) the control at r₅₀₀, where the
radial term plays no role, gives obs/pred  = 0.95 (rms 0.13 in ln ). At r₂₅₀₀ without a radial term
obs/pred is 0.26 (rms_(ln) 1.35, predicted f_(bar)(r₂₅₀₀)/f_(bar)(r₅₀₀) = 3.37); with the v4 law
0.45 (rms_(ln) 0.90, ratio 1.95, and one of the ten clusters has no solution because n > 1 inside
w ≃ 0.45); with the scale shift 0.84 (rms_(ln) 0.23, ratio 1.00). The observed ratio is 0.90. The v4
law, which scales the exponent as w^(−α), drives n beyond 1 at w < 1 for the highest ξ, where the
kernel no longer has a finite baryon fraction; the scale shift cannot do this because it multiplies
x rather than the exponent. At the criterion proposed in Paper I (0.10 dex), the v4 law with its
published α(ξ) fails on this sample (we did not re-optimise α).

X-COP out to r₂₀₀.

Ettori et al. (2019, Table 2) give f_(gas) and M_(HSE) at r₅₀₀ and r₂₀₀ for 12 clusters. After
adding stars (Kravtsov scaling, φ = 1) the ratio f_(bar)(r₂₀₀)/f_(bar)(r₅₀₀) is observed to be 1.03
(median); the scale shift with p = 0.5 predicts 0.98 (all 12) or 0.97 (the nine clusters left after
excluding the mergers A2319, A3266 and ZW1215), against 0.97 for the v4 law (rms of ln [obs/pred
ratio] 0.157 against 0.120 for the new term) and 0.42 without a radial term. The mass trend of this
ratio is  − 0.12 per dex observed and  − 0.11 predicted. The mass-dependent exponent p(ξ) ∝ ξ^(0.37)
(Sect. 8) predicts  + 0.11 and is disfavoured. At r₅₀₀ itself, the X-COP sample has obs/pred  = 1.17
(rms 0.19 in ln ) while the Vikhlinin sample gives 0.95 (Sect. 8).

Gain of the radial term.

Without the radial term the kernel overestimates the baryon fraction at r₂₅₀₀ by a factor 3–4
(obs/pred 0.27, rms_(ln) 1.27 over 12 clusters) and underestimates it at r₂₀₀ by a factor 2.4 (ratio
0.42 against 1.03). With p = 0.5 the rms falls to 0.225 at r₂₅₀₀ and 0.12 at r₂₀₀. In galaxies
ξ < ξ_(c), so s = 1 and the term is inactive: the gain belongs to groups and clusters. The test
shows that the term is necessary and that [Δ/Δ₅₀₀]^(p) describes it with one parameter; it does not
show that this is the only possible form, and p ≃ 0.5 was calibrated on samples that partially
overlap (A1795 and A2029 are in both Vikhlinin and X-COP; see Sect. 4.3 for the held-out
comparison).

Zero-point-free ratio test.

Dividing out the control at r₅₀₀ of each sample (where p plays no role:  − 0.05 ± 0.04 for Vikhlinin
and  + 0.16 ± 0.02 for Ettori) gives the mean of ln  of the ratio between the observed and the
predicted f_(bar)(r)/f_(bar)(r₅₀₀). At r₂₅₀₀ in ten Vikhlinin clusters:  − 1.29 ± 0.07 (no radial
term),  − 0.61 ± 0.21 (v4),  − 0.24 ± 0.06 (p = 0.45),  − 0.10 ± 0.06 (p = 0.5). At r₂₀₀ in nine
X-COP clusters:  + 0.87 ± 0.06,  + 0.02 ± 0.06,  + 0.10 ± 0.04,  + 0.03 ± 0.04 (errors are standard
errors over clusters). The new term is clearly better than v4 inside r₅₀₀ (Vikhlinin, groups, Allen,
where v4 loses solutions or diverges) and equivalent to it between r₅₀₀ and r₂₀₀, where v4 is
calibrated.

Dispersion of p.

The best-fit amplitudes range from 0.45 to 0.60 and their bootstrap intervals do not overlap (X-COP
0.40–0.50 against Ettori 0.60–0.65), so the scatter is systematic, not statistical: the five values
have mean 0.52 and standard deviation 0.07. Near r₂₅₀₀ and r₂₀₀ a change of 0.05 in p shifts the
predicted f_(bar) by about 0.13 in ln  (Vikhlinin:  − 0.28 →  − 0.15 from p = 0.45 to 0.50), so the
amplitude is known to  ∼  ± 20% in the predicted baryon fraction. It is not a constant of nature at
the level of the current data; sources are the hydrostatic bias (Sect. 8), the instrument zero
points, and the unmeasured stellar mass.

What is, and is not, held out.

p was fixed on X-COP before the other samples were evaluated, but the tests are not blind: variants
of the model (a mass-dependent p(ξ), Sect. 8) were evaluated on the same data, the Kravtsov stellar
treatment of the groups was adopted after the 9% case was inspected, and the samples overlap. A
blind test requires data not used here. The frozen prescription (parameters and code) is archived
with this paper (Appendix A) so that it can be applied to new samples before they are examined.

Evolution of g^(†)

We parametrise g^(†)(z) = g^(†)(0) E(z)^(α), with α = 1 the prediction g^(†) = cH/2π and α = 0 a
frozen scale. In clusters α enters both x and ξ, so a change of α moves the exponent as well as the
transition.

relative to the local baryonic Tully–Fisher relation are  − 0.44 (at z ≃ 0.9) and  − 0.27 (at
z ≃ 2.3), non-monotonic. The rms of ln (v_(pred)/v_(obs)) is 0.141 for α = 0, 0.113 (0.25), 0.094
(0.50), 0.097 (0.75) and 0.121 for α = 1. The galaxies therefore favour α ≃ 0.5–0.75 and the
clusters α ≳ 1. The two are reconciled only if the high-redshift galaxy systematics (gas masses from
scaling relations with 0.2 dex error and no atomic gas, pressure-support corrections of 0.1–0.2 dex,
selection that changes the stellar mass across bins) are of order 10% or more, which they are; with
three bins we cannot decide whether α depends on system scale. The dark matter fractions inferred in
z ≃ 2 galaxies are lower than at z = 0 (Übler et al. 2017; Genzel et al. 2017), whereas a larger
g^(†) at the same baryonic acceleration produces a larger K; this is the same tension seen from
another side and has not been tested quantitatively here.

Connecting galaxies to clusters: the ξ switch

The radial term cannot be applied to galaxies: in SPARC an exploratory s = (Δ/Δ_(out))^(p), with Δ
taken from the rotation curve itself, degrades the rms from 0.148 to 0.158, 0.235 and 0.468 dex for
p = 0.25, 0.5 and 1, and the global fit favours p ≃ 0.09. A switch based on the local density does
not separate the regimes, because inner cluster radii reach galactic densities; the discriminator
must be a property of the system. The compactness ξ is such a property: SPARC haloes have
ξ ≃ 0.004–0.094 (under two halo mass assumptions, A: M₅₀₀ = M_(b)/0.10; B: a stellar-to-halo
relation, so ξ for galaxies is imputed, not measured), the 43 groups ξ = 0.136–0.29, X-COP 0.34–0.54
and the Allen/Mantz clusters 0.40–0.85. No object lies in 0.094 < ξ < 0.136. A saturating switch
such as 1 − e^(−ξ/0.052), the form inherited from α(ξ) in Paper I, turns the term on partially at
galactic ξ and was found incompatible with the SPARC residuals.

independent units (13 clusters, 141 galaxies, 43 groups; weighting by points would let the 2803

groups lie at ξ < 0.16); the bracket of the first draft, 0.09 ≲ ξ_(c) ≲ 0.14, rested on the same

p(ξ) = p₀(ξ/0.4)^(q) without threshold, with as many parameters as a step, is disfavoured by
Δ(−2lnL) = 42–48 with respect to the step and 46–53 with respect to m = 4: the galaxies need p ≃ 0
and the clusters 0.45, and the groups lie between, which a power law cannot reproduce without
degrading the clusters (rms 0.024–0.026 against 0.016). The switch therefore behaves as a threshold
in ξ, of unknown width.

Dependence on the stellar mass of groups.

The group amplitude is set by the unmeasured stellar mass inside r₂₅₀₀. With Kravtsov stars
(φ = 0.7) the groups give p = 0.45 [0.39, 0.50], consistent with the clusters; with a stellar mass
of 9% of the gas (first draft) they give p = 0.80 [0.76, 0.85] and the joint fit then degrades the
clusters from 0.016 to 0.10 dex. The consistency of p across regimes therefore holds for the
Kravtsov relation and fails for the lower stellar masses, and groups cannot yet arbitrate.

Because ξ ∝ (M₅₀₀H)^(1/3) the switch predicts a redshift-dependent threshold mass whose ratio to the
z = 0 value, 0.57 at z = 1 and 0.34 at z = 2, is independent of ξ_(c):

  M₅₀₀^(thr) [M_(⊙)]      z = 0        z = 1        z = 2

  ξ_(c) = 0.09          4.2 × 10¹²   2.4 × 10¹²   1.4 × 10¹²
  ξ_(c) = 0.115         8.8 × 10¹²   5.0 × 10¹²   3.0 × 10¹²

For m = 4 the switch goes from 10% to 90% over a factor  ≃ 3 in ξ, i.e. about 27 in M₅₀₀, so the
thresholds are indicative; for the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation the corresponding zero-point shift
is Δlog v = 0.25log₁₀E(z) ( + 0.061 dex at z = 1,  + 0.118 at z = 2).

ΛCDM fits SPARC rotation curves better (0.087 or 0.059 against 0.142 dex), at the cost of one or two
free parameters per galaxy; the kernel has none, and equals the RAR in quality (0.148 against
0.144). In groups the outcome depends on the stellar mass, which is not measured: with the Kravtsov
relation the kernel (0.079–0.081 dex) is not distinguishable from ΛCDM with a c(M) prior and no free
parameter (0.089), whereas a stellar mass of 9% of the gas gives 0.131 at p = 0.5 and needs p = 0.8
(0.056). The groups therefore do not discriminate. In X-COP the radial term wins by AIC with a
margin of 74 to 94 over NFW + Duffy when only the parameters added to the fixed kernel are counted.
Charging also p, ξ_(c), m and A (four parameters) the margin is 66 with N = 39 and 34 with one
independent unit per cluster (N_(eff) = 13, same charge for NFW). Neither count is neutral: the 13
NFW parameters are per-cluster masses, whereas the kernel predicts masses from baryons but inherits
A from 59 systems that include these clusters; and the comparison is within the calibration sample
and with the Paper I baryon fraction. A positive offset of  + 0.15 dex appears in SPARC galaxies
with V_(max)<70 km s⁻¹ (312 points, rms 0.25), which we find not to depend on M_(bar)
and interpret as a selection effect of grouping by the observed maximum velocity. We therefore
describe the kernel as competitive in clusters and parsimonious in galaxies, not as superior in fit.

Tensions and tests that did not close

(1) Baryon fraction against mass at r₂₅₀₀.

The kernel predicts f_(bar)(r₂₅₀₀) rising with M₂₅₀₀ by  + 0.35 per dex, against a measured mass
slope of the observed fraction of  − 0.04 ± 0.09 (Allen) and  − 0.09 ± 0.07 (Mantz). In the
Vikhlinin sample ln (obs/pred) has a trend of  − 0.31 per dex of M₂₅₀₀ with p = 0.5 (median obs/pred
0.84, rms_(ln) 0.225 over twelve clusters) and  − 0.35 with p = 0.46 (0.76, 0.29). The effect
worsens with realistic stellar masses and is independent of the form of n(ξ).

(2) Absolute normalisation.

At r₅₀₀, where p plays no role, obs/pred is 0.95 for Vikhlinin (Chandra) and 1.17 for X-COP
(XMM-Newton), a  ∼ 20% difference that is larger than the radial effect under test; at r₂₀₀ the
X-COP value is 1.24. Known cross-calibration differences between the two instruments are of this
order and were not modelled. In terms of the ratio tests of Sect. 4.3 the two samples give
 − 0.10 ± 0.06 and  + 0.03 ± 0.04 for p = 0.5; their difference of 0.13 in ln  is of the size of the
zero-point offset and sets the scale of the cross-sample systematic.

(3) A mass-dependent exponent.

The form p(ξ) = p₀(ξ/0.4)^(q) with q > 0 (bootstrap q = 0.37 [0.31, 0.43], p₀ = 0.46 over Allen and
Mantz) removes the trend of tension (1) ( − 0.31 →  − 0.06, Fig. 3), but predicts a mass trend of
f_(bar)(r₂₀₀)/f_(bar)(r₅₀₀) of  + 0.11 against  − 0.12 observed and of the within-cluster r₂₅₀₀/r₅₀₀
ratio of  − 0.25 against  − 0.07. It is not supported and is discarded as a hypothesis; the simplest
reading remains constant p ≃ 0.5.

(4) Hydrostatic equilibrium and lensing.

All cluster masses but CLASH are hydrostatic. Hydrostatic masses measure g_(obs) without bias if the
gas is in equilibrium in the potential of Eq. (1), but non-thermal pressure (5.9% at r₅₀₀ and
10.5% at r₂₀₀ in X-COP) and departures from equilibrium were not modelled and the temperature and
density profiles predicted by the kernel were not computed. A mass-dependent bias of the sign needed
to close tension (1) would require hydrostatic masses to overestimate the true mass at high mass,
opposite to the usual expectation from non-thermal pressure. Weighing-the-Giants calibration finds
M_(L)/M_(X) = 0.96 (+0.06/−0.09) ± 9% (syst.) at r₂₅₀₀, with a mass index β = 0.78 (+0.17/−0.14)
(assuming M_(L) ∝ M_(X)^(β)), less than 2σ from 1 (Mantz et al. 2016); closing the trend would need
β ≃ 0.87. For the 12 clusters common to CLASH and Allen, M_(L)/M_(X)(r₂₅₀₀) has median 1.21
(weighted mean 1.09, scatter 0.35 in ln ), obs/pred is 0.93 with lensing masses and 1.10 with X-ray
masses (rms_(ln) 0.24 and 0.34). The lensing sample is small, high-mass and noisy, the gas was
rescaled to the lensing aperture assuming M_(gas) ∝ r^(1.2), and noise in M_(L) enters both axes, so
the mass trend itself is not informative there. Lensing calibration therefore reduces but does not
decide the matter.

(4b) Sensitivity to a hydrostatic bias.

We set M_(HSE) = (1−b)M_(true) and applied the kernel to M_(true). (a) X-COP, 39 points,
b(w) = b₅₀₀w^(κ) with κ = 0 or 1 and  − 0.1 ≤ b₅₀₀ ≤ 0.3: the best-fit p stays within 0.40–0.49; the
rms is 0.016–0.028 dex for the new term, 0.037–0.057 for v4 and 0.110–0.154 without radial term, so
the ordering of the three models does not depend on the bias. (b) With measured gas fractions
(f_(bar) = M_(gas)/M_(true)) the best-fit p rises with b: Vikhlinin (ratio test) 0.53 for b = 0,
0.56 for b₂₅₀₀ = 0.10–0.15, 0.60 for 0.20–0.25 and 0.63 for 0.30; Allen 0.48, 0.53, 0.57 and 0.60
for b =  − 0.1, 0, 0.1, 0.2. At fixed p = 0.5 the Vikhlinin ratio test moves from  − 0.10 ± 0.06 to
 − 0.21 ± 0.07 (b₂₅₀₀ = 0.15) and  − 0.33 ± 0.06 (0.20), while the v4 law degrades from  − 0.61 to
 − 1.2. A bias of 0.17, the value implied by the CLASH–Allen median M_(L)/M_(X) = 1.21, thus
corresponds to p ≃ 0.57, and the Mantz et al. calibration (M_(L)/M_(X) = 0.96, b ≃  − 0.04) to
p ≃ 0.52: both lie inside the sample-to-sample dispersion of Sect. 4.3. (c) A positive bias does not
remove the mass trend of tension (1): fitting b = b₀ + b₁(logM₂₅₀₀−14.4) to Allen requires
b₁ ≃  − 0.2 to  − 0.3 per dex, i.e. b(10¹⁵ M_(⊙)) ≃  − 0.12 to  − 0.21, hydrostatic masses
overestimating the true mass in the most massive clusters. The conclusion on the necessity of the
radial term and its ordering against v4 is robust to hydrostatic bias; the amplitude p and the mass
trend are not.

Sect. 5.

Extension to cosmological scales

The kernel applied to linear perturbations

For a top-hat baryonic perturbation δ_(b) of physical radius R,
g_(bar)=1/2Ω_b(a)H²δ_bR and

    x=(g_(bar))/(g^†)=π Ω_b(a) δ_b R/(c/H).

Over the history of the Universe and for R = 0.1–100 Mpc comoving, x = 10⁻⁸–4 × 10⁻³, always deep in
the low-acceleration regime. At z = 1100 with δ_(b) = 10⁻⁴, K = 7282, 2303, 729 and 231 for
R = 0.1, 1, 10, 100 Mpc, whereas mimicking cold dark matter needs K = Ω_(m)/Ω_(b) = 6.1. A toy
growth equation δ''+(2+dln H/dln a)δ'=3/2Ω_b K(x(δ))δ from
z = 1100 gives δ(z=0) = 123, 127 and 134 for δ_(i) = 10⁻⁵, 10⁻⁴, 10⁻³ at R = 10 Mpc comoving (it
scales as 1/R: 1.2 × 10³ at 1 Mpc, 13–15 at 100 Mpc), against 0.0076, 0.076 and 0.76 in ΛCDM. The
result is nearly independent of the initial amplitude (the force scales as δ^(1/2)), so the
primordial amplitude A_(s) would no longer control the spectrum and the fluctuations would not be
linear and Gaussian. The toy neglects photon pressure and baryon–photon coupling before decoupling,
which would delay growth, but the excess is 10³–10⁵.

Dilemma and damper

If the kernel acts on cosmological perturbations it overshoots; if it acts only on bound systems,
baryons alone grow by a factor 13 between z = 1100 and today against 760 in ΛCDM (δ = 0.013 instead
of 0.76 for δ_(i) = 10⁻³) and no galaxy forms, and the CMB acoustic peaks cannot be produced without
a potential well that does not couple to photons. A damper based on the baryon density contrast,

    K_(eff)=K_c (1-D)+K(x) D, D=expit[mln(ρ_b/ρ_c)/Δ_v], K_c=Ω_m/Ω_b≃6.12,

with Δ_(v) = 30 in ρ_(b)/ρ_(c) (total Δ ≃ 200) and m = 4 leaves bound systems unchanged and gives
δ(z=0) = 0.7604 for R = 1, 10 and 100 Mpc, identical to ΛCDM within 10⁻⁷ in the growth rate. This
equality holds by construction, since ρ_(b)/ρ_(c) ≲ 0.1 in the linear regime and D → 0: it is
consistent but not a test, and K_(c) is a postulate. A hard cap K ≤ K_(c) everywhere is excluded,
since at r₅₀₀ the kernel already gives K ≃ 7.5 > 6.12 (f_(bar) = 0.133 against the cosmic 0.163); a
cap would set f_(bar)(r₅₀₀) = 0.163 against the observed  ∼ 0.13. That clusters sit below the cosmic
baryon fraction without feedback is a qualitative consequence of K > K_(c) in bound systems; we have
not tested it against a specific measurement of the baryon depletion.

CMB

Reproducing the acoustic peaks requires a perturbation theory with baryon–photon coupling and a
relativistic completion; kernel-type enhancements of the baryon force do not reproduce the third
peak and the odd–even peak ratio, and the theories that do (e.g. Skordis & Złośnik 2021) add a field
that behaves as pressureless matter. We make no claim about the CMB or large-scale structure.

Discussion

What the program claims

1.  In bound systems with symmetric equilibrium the kernel, with λ, A, g^(†) fixed on galaxies and
    groups, describes the baryon fraction across four orders of magnitude in mass with one radial
    amplitude (p = 0.52 ± 0.07, the error being the dispersion between samples) and one switch in ξ
    (ξ_(c) ≃ 0.09–0.16, width m ≥ 3).

2.  The radial term [Δ/Δ₅₀₀]^(p) replaces the two-parameter exponent modulation, reduces the X-COP
    rms from 0.037 to 0.016–0.021 dex (in-sample) and, unlike the former, extends inside r₅₀₀ and,
    with p frozen, to r₂₅₀₀ and r₂₀₀ on held-out (not blind) data, with a predicted baryon fraction
    good to  ∼ 20% at the amplitude dispersion found.

3.  g^(†) ∝ H(z) is the preferred evolution in clusters and is not excluded in galaxies at z ≃ 1–2
    within current systematics.

This is a phenomenological statement of restricted scope, defensible from the data used and not
derived from a Lagrangian.

What the program does not claim

It does not claim that dark matter does not exist, nor that it replaces dark matter in cosmology:
the CMB, linear growth and primordial perturbations are untouched, and the damper of
the damper equation of Sect. 9.2 reproduces ΛCDM growth by postulate. It does not claim superiority in fit over
ΛCDM haloes, which fit SPARC better and, depending on the unmeasured stellar mass, may fit the
groups better. The tensions of Sect. 8 are open.

Comparison with competing approaches

Against MOND (Milgrom 1983) and the RAR (McGaugh et al. 2016) the transition scale evolves as
cH(z)/2π and the exponent depends on the compactness of the equilibrium; against ΛCDM the kernel is
a baryon-only prescription with no per-system halo parameters in the tested regimes, but without
cosmology. Observationally the two differ by the redshift evolution of the transition acceleration
and by the dependence of the baryon fraction on ξ and radius. Covariant relativistic MOND
completions (e.g. AeST) address the CMB by adding a field; the kernel does not.

Agenda for falsification

1.  Radial parameter. Resolved gas and lensing profiles (X-COP, eROSITA, XXL) should give
    p = 0.5 ± 0.1 independent of ξ and mass; given the present sample-to-sample dispersion of 0.07,
    a single sample outside 0.5 ± 0.15 is not decisive, but |p−0.5| > 0.15 in several independent
    samples or a significant mass dependence falsifies the scale shift. Apply the archived frozen
    prescription before looking at the data.

2.  Evolution of g^(†). Clusters at z > 0.5 with measured gas fractions (SPT, XMM) and larger
    samples of z ≃ 1–2 rotation curves should converge on a common α; independent samples preferring
    α < 0.5 at 2σ in clusters, or α > 1 in galaxies, falsify g^(†) ∝ H(z).

3.  The switch. Systems with 0.09 < ξ < 0.16 must show a transition of finite width (for m = 4, 10%
    to 90% over a factor 3 in ξ); galaxies with ξ > 0.16 should show s ≠ 1, and systems with
    ξ < 0.09 should show none. The threshold masses of Sect. 6 give the redshift dependence.

4.  Equilibrium in the kernel potential. Predict T(r) and n_(gas)(r) for the 12 X-COP and 12
    Vikhlinin clusters in the potential of Eq. (1) and compare with the measured profiles,
    including non-thermal pressure.

5.  Lensing at low mass. Per-cluster lensing masses (CCCP, LoCuSS, WtG) below M₂₅₀₀ ≃ 2 × 10¹⁴ M_(⊙)
    to decide the mass trend of Sect. 8; measured M_(*)(<r₂₅₀₀) in groups to fix φ.

6.  Systems not tested here. El Gordo (ξ ≃ 0.73) is an active merger and violates the equilibrium
    premise; ATLAS-3D/SLUGGS early-type galaxies and DESI rotation curves remain open from Paper I.

7.  Covariant formulation. Without an action the program has no predictions for gravitational waves,
    the CMB or strong fields.

Conclusions

The kernel of Paper I, extended with a single-amplitude radial term, describes bound systems from
galaxies to clusters better than before; it remains a phenomenological map, not a cosmological
theory.

Established.

1.  The radial term is necessary: without it the kernel mispredicts the baryon fraction at r₂₅₀₀ by
    a factor 3–4 and at r₂₀₀ by a factor 2.4.

2.  s = [Δ/Δ₅₀₀]^(p) with p ≃ 0.45–0.5 describes it with one amplitude: X-COP rms 0.126 →
    0.016–0.021 dex (v4 law: 0.037; in-sample), and with p = 0.5 frozen the ratio tests give
    ln (obs/pred) =  − 0.10 ± 0.06 at r₂₅₀₀ (Vikhlinin; v4:  − 0.61 ± 0.21) and  + 0.03 ± 0.04 at
    r₂₀₀ (Ettori; v4:  + 0.02 ± 0.06).

3.  The v4 law w^(−α(ξ)) is adequate for w ≳ 0.7 in X-COP but fails inside r₅₀₀ (n > 1) and does not
    meet the v4 criterion of 0.10 dex at r₂₅₀₀.

4.  g^(†) ∝ H(z) is preferred by Allen and Mantz gas fractions; a frozen g^(†) costs Δχ² = 57 and
    48.

5.  A threshold in ξ (ξ_(c) = 0.09–0.16, width m ≥ 3) connects galaxies, groups and clusters; a
    smooth power law p(ξ) is disfavoured by Δ(−2lnL) ≃ 50, and the threshold mass scales with
    redshift by 0.57 (z = 1) and 0.34 (z = 2).

Not established.

1.  The amplitude p is not a constant at the current precision: the best-fit values range from 0.45
    to 0.60 between samples (mean 0.52, standard deviation 0.07), a systematic of order 20% in the
    predicted baryon fraction.

2.  The sharpness and location of the galaxy–group switch are loosely constrained, and the group
    amplitude depends on an unmeasured stellar mass.

3.  The baryon fraction at r₂₅₀₀ rises with mass more than observed ( − 0.31 per dex in obs/pred);
    lensing calibration shifts it by  ∼ 1σ.

4.  The absolute normalisation differs by  ∼ 20% between the Chandra and XMM samples (0.95 against
    1.17 at r₅₀₀).

5.  A mass-dependent p(ξ) ∝ ξ^(0.37) closes the former but predicts the wrong sign of the
    r₅₀₀ → r₂₀₀ trend, and is discarded.

6.  Median KMOS^(3D) galaxies prefer g^(†) ∝ E^(0.5–0.75) against α ≳ 1 in clusters, within the
     ∼ 10% systematics of the former.

7.  Applied to linear perturbations the kernel boosts gravity 10²–10³ times; the damper recovers
    ΛCDM growth only through the postulated plateau K_(c) = Ω_(m)/Ω_(b); no CMB statement is made.

Position relative to ΛCDM.

In clusters the kernel is competitive (X-COP AIC  − 301 to  − 321 against  − 227, in-sample; margin
34–66 after charging p, ξ_(c), m, A); in galaxies it is parsimonious (zero parameters) but fits
worse than haloes (0.142 against 0.087 or 0.059 dex); in groups the kernel and ΛCDM are
indistinguishable with Kravtsov stellar masses and ΛCDM is better with lower ones; in cosmology the
kernel makes no competitive claim. The defensible claim is an empirical map of bound systems that
any covariant theory must reproduce in the weak-field stationary limit.

Practical value of parsimony.

The model has no per-system free parameter: given the baryonic profile and the equilibrium geometry
of a system, K_(eff) returns g_(obs) (or the baryon fraction) from five global numbers, λ and g^(†)
fixed on galaxies and A, p and the switch fixed on groups and clusters, all frozen in the archived
prescription. Three practical consequences follow, within the accuracy documented above. (i)
Forecasting without a halo fit. A rotation curve, a baryon fraction at r₂₅₀₀ or the threshold mass
at which a system leaves the galactic regime can be stated before the data are taken, with an error
budget (0.14 dex in galaxies,  ∼ 20% in the cluster baryon fraction), whereas an NFW description
needs one or two free parameters per galaxy (141–282 for the SPARC sample) or one mass per cluster,
and can absorb a mismatch that the kernel cannot. (ii) Cheap and auditable computation. Each
prediction is a single root-finding with no sampling, so it can be applied to large surveys or to
sparse and high-redshift data where halo parameters are unconstrained, and any discrepancy is
attributable to one of a handful of numbers rather than to a degenerate fit. (iii) Falsifiability.
The same scarcity of parameters is what exposed the tensions of Sect. 8 and the dispersion of p in
Sect. 4.3; each further sample either confirms the frozen values or breaks them. Parsimony is a
virtue only while the accuracy holds: the kernel fits galaxies worse than haloes (0.142 against
0.087 dex), the amplitude p carries a  ∼ 20% systematic, and it needs a measured baryonic profile as
input. Its practical role is therefore a first-order, reproducible estimator of dynamical masses in
bound systems, and a sharp test for competing descriptions, not a replacement of halo fitting where
precision is required.
